Where we start

Women are taking ownership of their sexual wellbeing

Not a claim about how much sex anyone is having — a real change in how openly women ask, and how much they expect to understand about their own bodies. Most of what gets sold to women in this category skips that understanding entirely. We would rather start with the question.

The distinction almost nobody explains

Desire, arousal and pleasure are three different things

They move independently. You can want intimacy and find your body slow to respond; you can be aroused and still find climax difficult. Knowing which one is actually low is what makes any plan work — and it is exactly why one product for everyone is a weak idea.

Desire

"Do I want intimacy?"

Shaped by stress, sleep, mood, hormones and how safe you feel — far more than by anything you can buy.

Arousal

"Is my body responding?"

Blood flow, lubrication and attention. It needs time and the right conditions, not more effort.

Pleasure

"Can I get there?"

A separate question again — and one where the right kind of stimulation matters more than most people are taught.

When it should be a doctor, not a purchase

We would rather lose a sale than have you manage something that needs proper care. Book the ₹99 consult — or see any registered doctor — if you have:

  • Pain during or after intimacy that keeps happening
  • Bleeding that is not your period
  • Unusual discharge, itching or a suspected infection
  • A sudden, unexplained change in desire or arousal
  • New symptoms after starting a medication or contraceptive
  • Anything postpartum that does not feel like it is settling
